WHAT GOES WRONG

  • No bookie. Someone has to hold the stakes, track who owes what and chase people. Nobody volunteers.
  • Vague terms. "Before half time": does stoppage time count? The argument about the bet replaces the bet.
  • No settlement. The result comes in, the loser goes quiet, and the bet is quietly forgotten.

WHY EXISTING OPTIONS DON'T FIT

  • Betting apps use real money and house odds, and won't price "minute of the first goal" for five mates.
  • Notes and spreadsheets need a volunteer accountant. See "no bookie".
Our angle: friends set the price themselves through an auction, and an engine holds the chips and settles.

HOW A BET WORKS

Four moves. No bookie.

01
PROPOSE
"First goal minute. 10 chips a lot."
02
BID IT DOWN
Jules offers a 12-minute window. Édouard says 8. Bon courage.
03
BE THE HOUSE
Tightest bid becomes market maker and picks the window: 35–43 min.
04
PICK A SIDE
Everyone else bets above 43 or below 35.
🎯
Goal lands inside the window? The house wins. The house is Édouard. Édouard is insufferable about it.
  • Propose. Anyone types a claim. The cat drafts the question, unit, bounds, stake per lot, payout mode and result source.
  • Bid it down. Friends bid the window width. Each bid must be narrower than the last, and the server closes the auction when the clock runs out.
  • Be the house. The tightest bidder becomes market maker and picks the low edge L. The high edge is H = L + width.
  • Pick a side. Everyone else bets above H or below L, 1 to 5 lots each.
  • Edges count as inside. A result exactly on L or H goes to the market maker.
Yes/no and stacked markets skip the auction and go straight to betting.

PAYOUT MODES

Pick your poison.

The creator picks how winners get paid.

BINARY
Right? Win your stake. Wrong? Lose it. Sleep well.
LINEAR
The more right you are, the more you win. Capped, so nobody bets the car.
LOG
Linear, for things that grow by ratio. Crypto bros, this one's yours.
POT
Everyone chips in. Winners split it. The pub classic.

Landing exactly on the edge of the window counts as inside. We don't make the rules. (We do. That's the rule.)

THE KNOBS

  • k is the stake per lot, w = H − L is the window width, and c caps how many widths a bet can win or lose (default 3).

PER LOT

  • Binary: win k or lose k.
  • Linear: k × (distance past the edge ÷ w), capped at ±c.
  • Log: the same, with distance measured on a log scale. Needs L > 0.
  • Pot: winners split the pot by lots. Inside the window, the market maker takes it.
Each player's worst case is locked in escrow up front, so nobody can owe more than they have.

LEDGER
Every chip movement, written down. Twice.
  • Chat. Messages are stored on the server and only recent history is kept. Empty and oversized messages are rejected.
  • Results. At the resolve time the cat looks up the published result, cites its sources and proposes it. Nothing settles until a 30-minute challenge window passes.
  • The web. Gemini with Google Search finds the real event behind a bet, briefs every bidder, and shows outside odds. No sources, no number. Links must be https, and lookups are cached to save quota.
  • Replays. For tests and dev, recorded matches (goals, VAR, half time) replay at any speed, the same way every time.
  • Ledger. Every chip movement is a wallet or escrow entry in Postgres. Redis carries sessions and live updates.

The AI can't move a single chip. It suggests; the engine decides. No API key? The cat says it's off duty. It never makes things up.

WHAT THE AI CAN'T DO

  • Move chips. Agents return proposals only; the deterministic engine is the only thing that changes balances.
  • Run a strategy you didn't confirm. Typed or spoken strategies are blocked until you confirm all three: what you're doing, your take-profit, and your loss floor.
  • Make things up. No sources, no number: outside odds, briefings and results all need web sources behind them.
  • Guess. If a message isn't clearly a bet, the cat asks a question instead of inventing a market.
  • Settle on a hunch. It only proposes an in-range result backed by sources, then waits out a 30-minute challenge window.

WHAT THE ENGINE ENFORCES

  • Worst-case loss is escrowed before a bet is accepted. Max 1 to 5 lots per take.
  • Idempotency keys, so a double tap can't bet twice.
  • The gateway ties every action to the signed-in player. You can't bet as someone else.
  • A referee can't trade in their own market, and a countdown locks bets at zero.
  • Manual results on bot-settled bets, and the dev and demo routes, are blocked in production.
No API key? The cat says it's unavailable and never invents a market, a fact or a result.

  • The spec has a worked example computed by hand: Ana, Ben, Cat and Dan, window 35–43, a mid-match lot sale at 20 CHIP. The engine matches every payout for results of 50 and 39.

THE TESTS

  • Property tests: each run generates 60 random window markets across all 4 payout modes, plus 40 random withdrawal and stop-loss sequences, and checks that chips are never created or lost, nobody goes negative, and replays are identical.
  • End-to-end: the full Arsenal match, VAR included, replayed through the engine.
  • Risk gate: strategies missing a take-profit or a loss floor are blocked.
  • Live agents: 22 tests: no key means no invented market, non-bets get a question, results need evidence and a challenge window, and repeat checks don't burn quota.
  • Gateway: 18 tests for login, sessions, player binding and WebSocket auth.
Not benchmarked yet: how accurately the AI reads chat messages. Next step: a labelled set of real messages (bets vs. chatter), scoring how often Gemini spots the bet and finds the right event.
